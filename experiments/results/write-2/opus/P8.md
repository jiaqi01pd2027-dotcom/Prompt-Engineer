Six months ago we moved Northstar Mentors from a five-day week to a four-day week. Fridays off, same pay. We are ten people, fully remote: six mentors, two in operations, two founders. We ran a fixed three-month trial, looked at what happened, and extended it.

What went up: mentoring sessions delivered per person per week, by about 8 percent. Team satisfaction went up in our internal survey, and nobody has left. Those are the two results I trust most.

Revenue was flat compared with the six months before the switch. Flat, not up. If you are hoping a shorter week grows the top line by itself, this is not that story. The problems were also smaller than we planned for. Coordinating with clients on Fridays needed a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply. That was the whole list.

Three things I would tell another founder. Decide the metric you will watch before you start, because afterwards everyone finds the number that flatters them. Keep the trial fixed-length, so it can end without anyone losing face. And do not sell it internally as a perk. A perk can be taken away, and your team knows that. Treat it as an operating change: same output, fewer days, and a plan for who covers Fridays.

If you have tried a four-day week, what did your numbers actually show?
