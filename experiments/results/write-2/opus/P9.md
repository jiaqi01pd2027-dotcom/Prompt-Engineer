Six months ago we moved Northstar Mentors to a four-day week. Fridays off, same pay. We are ten people, fully remote: six mentors, two on operations, two founders. We ran a three-month trial first, then extended it.

Here is what actually happened.

Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week went up about 8%. That was the metric we picked before we started, and it is the only number I would put weight on.

Revenue was flat compared with the six months before the switch. Not up. We did not make more money, and I would rather say that plainly than dress it up as a leading indicator.

Team satisfaction rose in our internal survey, and nobody has left.

The problems were the ones we expected. Clients sometimes needed us on a Friday. A shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply covered it. That was the whole operational cost.

Three things I would tell another founder thinking about it.

Decide the metric before you start. If you pick it afterwards, you will pick the one that looks good.

Keep the trial fixed-length. An open-ended trial is just a change you have not admitted to, and it is far harder to reverse.

Do not sell it internally as a perk. A perk can be withdrawn, and people know it. Treat it as an operating change and hold it to the same standard as any other change to how the work gets done.

Six months is not long enough to call this settled, and we are still watching.

If you tried a four-day week and went back, what broke?
