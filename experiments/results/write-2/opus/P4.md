Six months ago Northstar Mentors, a fully remote developer-mentorship company of ten people, moved from a five-day week to a four-day week. Fridays off, same pay. We ran it as a three-month trial first, then extended it.

Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week went up about 8 percent. That is the one number we have. It is not dramatic, but it moved in the right direction and it has stayed there. Satisfaction in our internal survey rose as well, and nobody has left since the change.

Revenue is flat compared with the six months before the switch. We did not expect a jump and we did not get one. The problems were the ones we anticipated and they were small: coordinating with clients who work Fridays needed a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply. That was the entire list.

Three things we would tell another founder. Decide before you start which metric you will watch, and write it down, because afterwards everyone will read the same six months differently. Keep the trial to a fixed length so it ends on a date rather than on a mood. And do not sell it internally as a perk. A perk is something that can be withdrawn, and it invites gratitude rather than problem-solving. We framed it as an operating change: the same commitments, four days to meet them. That framing did more work than anything else we did.
