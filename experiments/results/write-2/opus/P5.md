Six months ago we moved Northstar Mentors from a five-day to a four-day week. Fridays off, same pay, for all ten of us: six mentors, two people in operations, and the two founders. We ran it as a three-month trial first, then extended it.

Output went up rather than down. Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week are about 8 percent higher than before the switch. That is the one number we committed to tracking, and it is the only productivity claim we will make. Our internal satisfaction survey also improved, and nobody has left.

Revenue is flat, level with the six months before the change. We had quietly hoped for some lift and did not get one. The problems were the ones we expected. Clients occasionally needed us on a Friday, which took a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply to solve. That was the whole of it.

Three things we would tell another founder. Decide the metric you will watch before you start, or you will spend month four arguing about impressions. Keep the trial fixed-length and say the end date out loud, so stopping stays a real option rather than an admission of failure. And do not sell it internally as a perk. We framed it as an operating change, and the team treated it as something to make work rather than something to defend.
