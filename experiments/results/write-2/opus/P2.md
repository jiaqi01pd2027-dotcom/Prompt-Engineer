Six months ago we cut our week to four days. Fridays off, same pay. No manifesto, no announcement post.

Northstar Mentors is 10 people, fully remote: six mentors, two in operations, two founders. We ran a three-month trial first, then extended it.

What the numbers say at the six-month mark:

Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week are up about 8%.

Revenue is flat against the six months before the switch.

Internal satisfaction is up. Nobody has left.

The problems were the ones we expected, and smaller. Clients occasionally needed us on a Friday. We handled it with a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply. That was the entire fix.

Flat revenue is the part people push on. We think it's fine. We didn't do this to grow faster this quarter. We did it to find out whether the same output fits in less time. It does. What we do with the room that opens up is a separate decision.

Three things I'd tell another founder:

Decide your metric before you start. Ours was sessions per person per week. Without it, we'd have spent six months arguing about how it felt.

Keep the trial fixed-length. An open-ended experiment is just a change you're too nervous to name, and it gets judged on its worst week.

Don't sell it internally as a perk. Perks get taken away, and people plan around that. We introduced it as an operating change with the same expectations attached. That framing did more work than anything else we tried.

If you've looked at this and decided against it, what stopped you?
