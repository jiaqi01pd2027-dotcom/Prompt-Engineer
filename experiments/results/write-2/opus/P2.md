Six months ago we moved Northstar Mentors to a four-day week. Fridays off, same pay. Here is what actually happened.

We are 10 people, fully remote: six mentors, two on operations, two founders. We ran a three-month trial, then extended it.

Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week went up about 8%. Revenue is flat against the six months before the switch. Our internal satisfaction survey went up, and nobody has left.

The problems were the ones we expected. Clients occasionally needed us on a Friday. We added a note on the shared calendar and a clear auto-reply. That was the whole fix. I wish I had a better story there.

Three things I would tell another founder thinking about it:

Decide the metric before you start. We picked sessions delivered per mentor per week. If we had waited to see what the data said, we would have found a number that made us look good.

Keep the trial fixed-length. Three months, with a real decision at the end. Open-ended trials never end and never get judged.

Do not sell it internally as a perk. We called it an operating change, because that is what it is. A perk gets withdrawn when things get tight, and everyone knows it, so nobody plans around it. An operating change gets defended.

The honest summary: same revenue, a bit more output, a team that wants to stay. That is not a revolution. It was still worth doing.

If you have considered this and not done it, what stopped you?
