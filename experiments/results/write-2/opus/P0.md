Six months ago we moved Northstar Mentors to a four-day week. Fridays off, same pay. Here is what the numbers actually say.

We are 10 people, fully remote: six mentors, two in operations, two founders. We ran a fixed three-month trial, reviewed it, then extended.

After six months:

Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week are up about 8%.
Revenue is flat against the six months before the switch.
Internal satisfaction scores are up, and nobody has left.

Flat revenue is the honest headline. This did not grow the business. We get the same output from four days that we used to get from five, and the team is meaningfully happier about it. That was the trade we were prepared to make, and we said so before we started.

The problems were the ones we expected and smaller. Clients who work Fridays needed a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply. That was the entire fix.

Three things I would tell another founder:

Decide the metric you will watch before you start. Choose it afterwards and you will choose the one that flatters you.

Keep the trial a fixed length. An open-ended trial is just a change nobody has agreed to review.

Do not sell it internally as a perk. A perk is a favour that can be withdrawn, and people treat it like one. Frame it as an operating change and the team takes the constraint seriously, because the constraint is the point.
