Six months ago we moved Northstar Mentors to a four-day week. Fridays off, same pay. Ten of us, fully remote: six mentors, two on operations, two founders. We ran a fixed three-month trial first, then extended it.

Founders ask us about this roughly once a week, usually after reading a version with better numbers than ours. Here are ours.

Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week went up about 8%. Revenue is flat against the six months before the switch. Internal satisfaction is up, and nobody has left.

The caveat we'd want if we were the ones reading this: with ten people and six months of data, we can't prove the four-day week caused the 8%. It's what we measured, not proof of anything.

The problems were smaller than expected. Clients needed to know Fridays were gone. A shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply covered it. That was the operational cost, in full.

Three things I'd tell another founder. Decide the metric before you start; pick it afterwards and you'll pick the one that flatters you. Keep the trial fixed-length, because an open-ended trial is a decision you never actually make. And don't sell it internally as a perk. A perk can be withdrawn, and people know it. Treat it as an operating change and hold it to operating standards.

Flat revenue and a team that wants to stay is a trade we'd make again.

If you've run a trial like this: what metric did you watch, and did it actually move?
