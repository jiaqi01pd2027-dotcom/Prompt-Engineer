## Reasoning

**What sceptical founders actually want to know.** Did revenue drop? Did clients get annoyed? Does this survive at 10 people, where every person is load-bearing and there's no slack to absorb a lost day? And: what did it cost you that you're not saying?

**Which facts carry it.** Flat revenue is the strongest fact in the sheet precisely because it's unflattering — it buys credibility for everything else and pre-empts the "sure, but did sales suffer" reply. The 8% per-person lift is the concrete counterweight. The Friday coordination fix is good because it's boringly small and specific. The three pieces of advice are the reusable part.

**Filler.** The 6/2/2 headcount breakdown. "Satisfaction up in our internal survey" — every such post says this and no reader believes a self-run survey; keep it only if it's flagged as weak evidence. "No problems we didn't expect" as a standalone claim is worthless without the example.

**What would make it generic.** Rest-as-productivity sermonising, "we did the impossible," a deep-work tangent, breathless framing of flat revenue as secretly a win, and a closing "would you try it?" — which invites agreement, not information.

**The question.** Ask the sceptics, not the fans, and force a number: what would have to go wrong, measurably, for you to reverse it. Anyone who can't answer has just learned why they haven't started.

## Post:

Six months ago we moved Northstar Mentors to a four-day week. Fridays off, same pay. Here are the numbers, including the one that didn't move.

Sessions delivered per mentor per week: up about 8%.
Revenue: flat against the previous six months.
Nobody has left.
Internal satisfaction: up — though I'd treat any survey we run on ourselves with suspicion.

The flat revenue is the part worth sitting with. We didn't grow because of this. We bought back a day a week for ten people and grew no faster over those six months. We'd make that trade again, but it is a trade, not a free lunch.

The problems were smaller than we'd budgeted for. Clients occasionally needed us on a Friday. A shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply covered it. That was the entire operational cost, and nobody has raised it since month one.

Three things I'd tell another founder:

Pick the metric before you start. Choose it afterwards and you'll choose the flattering one.

Fix the trial length and say it out loud. Ours was three months, then we extended it deliberately. An open-ended trial is just a change nobody has committed to.

Don't sell it internally as a perk. Perks get withdrawn when things get hard. We called it an operating change, so the team treated protecting it as their problem rather than a gift they had to be grateful for.

For founders who've considered this and passed: what number would have to move, and by how much, before you'd call it off? If you can't name it, that may be why you haven't started.
