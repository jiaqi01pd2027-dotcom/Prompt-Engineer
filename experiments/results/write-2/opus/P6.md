Six months ago we moved our ten-person mentorship company to a four-day week. Fridays off, same pay. We ran it as a three-month trial, then extended it.

The numbers, six months in: mentoring sessions delivered per person per week are up about 8%. Revenue is flat against the six months before the switch. Internal satisfaction is up and nobody has left.

The problems were the ones we expected, and no others. Clients occasionally need us on a Friday. We fixed that with a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply. That was the whole fix. I keep waiting for the harder problem and it has not arrived.

Three things I would tell another founder.

Decide the metric before you start. We picked sessions per person per week. Settling it in advance meant we spent the trial arguing about how to work, not about whether it was working.

Keep the trial fixed-length. Three months, with a real decision at the end. An open-ended trial is a change you are too nervous to name.

Do not sell it internally as a perk. We called it an operating change, which carries an obligation: the same work in less time. I think that framing is why it held.

Flat revenue is still flat revenue. I would rather be growing. But the same money with six mentors who are not burned out is a trade I understand.
