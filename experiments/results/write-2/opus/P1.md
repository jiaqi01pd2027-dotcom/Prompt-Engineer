Six months ago we moved Northstar Mentors to a four-day week. Fridays off, same pay.

We are ten people, all remote: six mentors, two on operations, two founders. We ran a three-month trial first, then extended it.

Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week are up roughly 8%. Revenue is flat against the six months before the change. Our internal satisfaction survey went up, and nobody has left.

The problems were the ones we expected. Clients occasionally need us on a Friday. We handled it with a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply. That was the whole fix.

Three things I would tell another founder considering this:

Decide your metric before you start. We picked sessions delivered per person per week, and agreed what a bad result would look like. Without that, you argue about vibes at the end of the trial.

Keep the trial fixed-length. Ours was three months with a date on the calendar to decide. An open-ended trial is a change you have not committed to, and people can feel it.

Do not sell it internally as a perk. We framed it as an operating change: same commitments to clients, less time to meet them, so we had to cut things. The team took it seriously because we did.

I want to be careful about what this proves. Ten people, one business model, six months. Revenue flat is not revenue up. But it has cost us nothing we can measure, and the team is in better shape.

What metric would you need to see move before you would try it?
