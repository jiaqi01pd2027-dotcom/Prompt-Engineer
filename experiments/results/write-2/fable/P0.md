Six months ago Northstar Mentors moved to a four-day week. Fridays off, same pay, all ten of us. Here is what the numbers say now that we have some distance from it.

Some context first. We are a fully remote developer-mentorship company: six mentors, two people in operations, two founders. We ran a three-month trial, extended it, and then made it permanent.

What changed after six months:

Productivity: mentoring sessions delivered per person per week are up about 8%. That is the metric we chose to watch before we started, and it is the one we would have used to reverse the decision.

Revenue: flat compared with the six months before the switch. Not up. We are not going to pretend otherwise.

Team: satisfaction is up in our internal survey, and nobody has left.

Problems: none we did not see coming. Clients occasionally wanted Friday meetings. A shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply handled it. That was the whole list.

If another founder asked us how to approach this, we would say three things.

Decide the metric you will watch before you start. If you pick it afterwards, you will pick the one that makes the decision look right.

Keep the trial fixed-length. An open-ended trial is just a policy you have not admitted to yet.

Do not sell it internally as a perk. Perks get taken away when things get tight. Treat it as an operating change, with the same scrutiny you would give a pricing change or a new tool, and the team will treat it that way too.

We are ten people, so take the sample size for what it is. But we went in expecting a productivity hit and got a small gain, and expecting friction with clients and got almost none.
