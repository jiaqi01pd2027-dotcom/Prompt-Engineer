Six months ago we moved Northstar Mentors to a four-day week. Fridays off, same pay. Ten people, fully remote: six mentors, two in operations, two founders. Here is what happened.

Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week went up about 8%. Revenue is flat compared with the six months before. Team satisfaction is up in our internal survey, and nobody has left.

The problems were the ones we expected. Clients sometimes wanted Fridays. A note in the shared calendar and a clear auto-reply solved that. Nothing else came up.

I want to be careful here. Flat revenue is not a win. It is the absence of a loss, and it came with 20% fewer working hours. That is the actual result, and I would rather say it plainly than dress it up.

Three things I would tell another founder:

Decide the metric before you start. We picked sessions per person per week. Without that, you end up arguing about whether it "feels" like it is working, and everyone's feelings will differ.

Keep the trial fixed-length. Ours was three months. We extended it on purpose, at the end, with the numbers in front of us. An open-ended trial is just a policy you have not admitted to yet.

Do not sell it internally as a perk. Perks get cut when things get tight. We treated it as an operating change, with the same expectations for output, and that framing has held up.

Would we go back? Not on these numbers. But we are ten people with a simple service model, and I do not know how this holds at fifty.
