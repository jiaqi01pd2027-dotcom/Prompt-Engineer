Six months ago we moved Northstar Mentors to a four-day week. Fridays off, same pay. We are ten people, fully remote: six mentors, two in operations, two founders. We ran it as a three-month trial first, then extended it.

The numbers so far. Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week are up about 8%. Revenue is flat compared with the six months before the switch. Satisfaction is up in our internal survey, and nobody has left. Ten people is a small sample, so I would not put the 8% on a slide, but it did not go down, which was the thing I was afraid of.

The problems were the ones we expected. Clients still work Fridays, so we added a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply. That was it.

Three things I would tell another founder. Decide the metric you will watch before you start, because if you pick it afterward you will pick the one that makes you look right. Keep the trial fixed-length; an open-ended trial is a policy you have not admitted to yet. And do not sell it internally as a perk. A perk can be taken away, and everyone knows it. An operating change gets measured, argued over, and adjusted, which is what you actually want.

Flat revenue on 20% fewer working days is not a headline. It is a trade we understand and would make again, and the honest version is that we are still watching it.
