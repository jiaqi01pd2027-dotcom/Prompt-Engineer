Six months ago Northstar Mentors moved from a five-day week to a four-day week. Fridays off, same pay, all ten of us: six mentors, two in operations, two founders. We ran it as a three-month trial first, then extended it. We are fully remote, which made the mechanics easier than they would be for most.

What went up: mentoring sessions delivered per person per week, by about 8%. That surprised me. I expected to hold output steady at best. Team satisfaction went up in our internal survey, and nobody has left.

What stayed flat: revenue. It is level with the six months before the switch. More sessions did not turn into more money, at least not yet, and I would be lying if I said otherwise. Problems were the ones we expected. Clients emailing on a Friday needed a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply. That was the whole fix.

What I would tell another founder: pick the metric you will watch before you start, not after. Keep the trial a fixed length so nobody is guessing when the decision gets made. And do not sell it internally as a perk. A perk can be taken away, and everyone knows it. Treat it as an operating change and run it like one.
