Six months ago, Northstar Mentors moved from a five-day to a four-day work week. Fridays off, same pay. We are a fully remote developer-mentorship company of ten people: six mentors, two in operations, and two founders. We started with a fixed three-month trial, extended it, and have now been on four days for six months. Here is what we have seen.

Productivity went up. Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week rose about 8%.

Revenue was flat. Compared with the six months before the switch, it did not move. We are not going to dress that up. Fewer days did not cost us revenue, but they did not add any either.

Team satisfaction rose in our internal survey, and nobody has left.

Problems: none we did not expect. Coordinating with clients on Fridays needed a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply. That was the whole fix.

If you are a founder weighing the same move, three things we would tell you:

1. Decide the metric you will watch before you start. Without one, every conversation about whether it is working becomes a matter of opinion.

2. Keep the trial fixed-length. A trial with no end date is a policy nobody has actually agreed to.

3. Do not sell it internally as a perk. Treat it as an operating change. Perks get cut when things get tight. Operating changes get measured and adjusted.

Six months in, we are keeping it. Output up, revenue flat, team intact.

If you run a small company, which metric would you watch before trying this?
