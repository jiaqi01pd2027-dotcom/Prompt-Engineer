Six months ago, Northstar Mentors moved from a five-day to a four-day work week: Fridays off, same pay, for all ten of us. We started with a three-month trial, then extended it.

The thing we were most nervous about, output, went the other way. Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week rose by about 8%. Team satisfaction went up in our internal survey, and nobody has left. We are a small team, so I would not read too much into any one figure, but the direction was clear and it has held.

Revenue was flat compared with the six months before the switch. Not up, not down. The problems we hit were the ones we expected. Coordinating with clients on Fridays took a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply, and that was about it. We are fully remote and were already used to working across time zones, which probably made this easier for us than it would be for some.

Three things I would tell another founder. Decide the metric you will watch before you start, not after, or you will end up arguing about what counts. Keep the trial fixed-length, with a date when you sit down and decide. And do not sell it internally as a perk. Treat it as an operating change, with the same expectations you would attach to any other one. That framing shaped how the team approached it more than anything else we did.
