Six months ago Northstar Mentors, a 10-person fully remote developer-mentorship company, moved from a 5-day week to a 4-day week. Fridays off, same pay. We ran a 3-month trial first, then extended it. Here is what actually happened.

Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week went up, about 8%. Team satisfaction went up in our internal survey, and nobody has left. That is the full list of things that improved. It is a short list, but both items are real and both held for six months.

Revenue was flat. Not up, not down, flat compared with the six months before the switch. The problems we hit were the ones we expected. Coordinating with clients on Fridays needed a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply. That was it. If you are waiting for the part where the numbers jumped, there is no such part.

If you are thinking about doing this, three things we would do again, and one we would do sooner. Decide the metric you will watch before you start, not after, or you will grade yourself on whatever looks best. Keep the trial fixed-length, so the decision to continue is a real decision and not a drift. And do not sell it internally as a perk. Treat it as an operating change, because that is what it is, and a perk is much harder to take back if the trial fails.
