Six months ago Northstar Mentors moved to a four-day week. Fridays off, same pay, all ten of us: six mentors, two in operations, two founders. We ran a fixed three-month trial, extended it, then made it permanent. Here is what the numbers say now.

Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week are up about 8 percent. Revenue is flat against the six months before the switch. Satisfaction in our internal survey is up, and nobody has left.

Problems: nothing we did not expect. Some clients wanted us on Fridays. A shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply took care of it. That was the whole cost on the client side.

I want to be careful with these results. An 8 percent bump in a ten-person company is a small number, and flat revenue is not growth. What we can say is that we cut a fifth of our working days, lost no output and no money, and the team is happier.

Three things we would tell another founder:

Decide the metric you will watch before you start. Ours was sessions per person per week. Pick it afterwards and you will pick whichever one makes the trial look good.

Keep the trial fixed-length. An open-ended trial is not a trial, it is a policy you have not admitted to yet.

Do not sell it internally as a perk. Perks get taken away when things get tight. We treated it as an operating change, with the same scrutiny as any other, and that is why the team trusted it.
