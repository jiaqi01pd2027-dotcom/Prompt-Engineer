Six months ago we moved Northstar Mentors from a five-day week to a four-day week. Fridays off, same pay. We are a fully remote developer-mentorship company with ten people: six mentors, two in operations, and two founders. We ran a three-month trial first, then extended it.

Here is what six months looks like.

Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week went up by about 8%. Revenue is flat compared with the six months before the switch. Team satisfaction went up in our internal survey, and nobody has left.

So the team is delivering slightly more in less time, and the business is making the same money. That is the honest picture. It is not a growth story, and I would be wary of anyone telling you a four-day week is one.

The problems were the ones we expected. Coordinating with clients on Fridays took a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply. Nothing else broke.

Three things I would tell another founder considering this:

Decide the metric you will watch before you start. If you pick it afterwards, you will pick the one that flatters the result.

Keep the trial fixed-length. An open-ended trial is a decision you have not made yet, and the team knows it.

Do not sell it internally as a perk. Treat it as an operating change. Perks get quietly revoked when things get tight; operating changes get measured and defended.
