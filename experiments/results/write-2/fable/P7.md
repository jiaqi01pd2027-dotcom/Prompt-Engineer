**Reasoning**

- *What sceptical founders want:* numbers with a denominator, what it cost, who left, what broke, and whether it can be undone. They distrust posts where everything went up.
- *Facts that carry the post:* revenue flat (the credibility anchor, because most posts claim it rose), sessions per person up ~8%, zero attrition, the 3-month fixed trial, "treat it as an operating change." Filler: the team breakdown (6/2/2), "satisfaction up" with no number, the tone instructions.
- *What would make it generic:* a "here's what I learned" hook, a listicle of lessons with bold headers, claiming happier people and more output with no trade-off, ending with "would you try it?"
- *Question that gets an honest reply:* not a yes/no. Ask for a concrete number, and frame it around stopping rather than starting, since founders think harder about exits.

**Post:**

Six months ago Northstar Mentors moved to a four-day week. Fridays off, same pay, ten people, fully remote. Here is what the numbers say, including the one that will not make a headline.

Revenue is flat. Same as the six months before the switch. If you are hoping a shorter week pays for itself in new business, we cannot show you that.

What did move: mentoring sessions delivered per person per week rose about 8%. Our internal satisfaction survey went up and nobody has left. The problems were the ones we predicted. Clients reaching us on a Friday needed a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply. That was the whole fix.

So the honest summary is: same money, more output per head, a team that stays. For a ten-person company where replacing a mentor takes months, that last part matters more than it sounds.

Three things we would tell another founder.

Pick the metric you will watch before you start. Ours was sessions per person per week. Without it, the debate at the end of the trial is about feelings.

Keep the trial fixed-length. We ran three months, then chose to extend. An open-ended trial never ends, so it never gets evaluated either.

Do not sell it internally as a perk. A perk gets quietly withdrawn when a quarter goes badly. An operating change gets measured and defended.

If you tried this and could only watch one number, which one would tell you it was time to stop?
