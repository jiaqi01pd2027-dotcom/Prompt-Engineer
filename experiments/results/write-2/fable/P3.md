Six months ago Northstar Mentors moved to a four-day week. Fridays off, same pay, all ten of us. Another founder asks me about it roughly once a week, and most have only heard the hype version, so here is the plain one.

What changed

Productivity went up, modestly. Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week rose about 8%. Not the 40% you may have read about elsewhere.

Revenue was flat against the six months before. Flat is a real result: we removed 20% of working days and lost nothing. But nobody should expect a four-day week to grow the top line on its own.

Satisfaction rose in our internal survey and nobody has left. With ten people, one departure would have changed that sentence, so I hold the number lightly.

What it cost

Less than we feared. The only friction was coordinating with clients on Fridays, solved with a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply. That was it.

What we would tell another founder

Decide the metric you will watch before you start. Pick it afterwards and you will choose whatever looks best.

Keep the trial fixed-length. Ours was three months, and we still extended it once before committing. Looking back, the extension was hesitation dressed up as rigour.

Do not sell it internally as a perk. Perks get withdrawn when times are hard. Treat it as an operating change and hold it to the same standard as a pricing change.

We are ten people, fully remote, in one business. Your results may differ.

If you have tried this, or decided against it, what was the metric that settled it for you?
