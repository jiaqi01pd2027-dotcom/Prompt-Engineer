Northstar Mentors is a 10-person, fully remote developer-mentorship company — 6 mentors, 2 operations, 2 founders. Six months ago we moved from a five-day to a four-day work week: Fridays off, same pay. We ran a three-month trial first, then decided to extend it.

Here's what actually happened, without the spin.

Mentoring sessions delivered per person, per week, went up about 8%. Team satisfaction rose in our internal survey, and nobody has left since we started. Those numbers made the change feel worth it.

Revenue was flat. Not up, not down. I want to say that plainly, because most posts about shorter work weeks bury the flat number under the productivity gain, and founders weighing this deserve the honest version: over six months, the shorter week did not grow our top line.

The problems were the ones we expected, nothing more. The only real friction was coordinating with clients on Fridays, and a shared calendar note plus a clear auto-reply took care of it.

If you're a founder or CEO thinking about trying this, here's what we'd tell you. Decide the metric you're going to watch before you start, not after. Keep the trial a fixed length, so you're not tempted to extend it just because morale feels good in the moment. And don't sell it internally as a perk — treat it as an operating change you'll actually evaluate, because that's what tells you whether to keep it or roll it back.

We kept it. Six months in: flat revenue, higher output per person, and a team that's staying.

Would you trade flat revenue for a happier, more productive team, or does growth have to come first?
