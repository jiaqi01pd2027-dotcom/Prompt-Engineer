Six months ago we moved Northstar Mentors, our 10-person remote developer-mentorship team, from five days to four. Same pay, Fridays off. We ran it as a fixed three-month trial first, then decided to keep it. Here is what actually happened, not the version you hear at conferences.

Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week went up, about 8 percent. Team satisfaction in our internal survey went up too, and nobody has left since we made the change. Those are the numbers most people want to hear.

Revenue was flat compared with the six months before the switch. Not up, not down. I am not going to bury that in a subordinate clause or spin it into a win — it is flat, and that is the honest answer.

The problems we hit were the ones we expected and nothing more. Clients occasionally forgot we were out on Fridays, so we added a line to our shared calendar and a clear auto-reply. That solved it.

If you are a founder thinking about this, three things mattered more than anything else. Decide the one metric you will actually watch before you start, so you are not relitigating the decision every week based on vibes. Keep the trial a fixed length and set the end date in advance. And do not sell it to your team as a perk — treat it as an operating change you are testing, because that is what it is.

We are six months in and still on four days.

What would you need to see in your own numbers before you would try it?
