Northstar Mentors is a 10-person, fully remote developer-mentorship company: six mentors, two operations, two founders. Six months ago we moved from a five-day week to a four-day week, Fridays off, same pay, after running a fixed three-month trial that we then decided to extend.

Here is what went up. Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week rose about 8 percent. Our internal satisfaction survey scores went up too, and nobody has left the company since the switch.

Here is what did not move: revenue. It was flat compared with the six months before we made the change. We are stating that plainly because most posts on this topic skip it. On problems, we did not hit anything we hadn't already planned for. The one real friction point was coordinating with clients on Fridays, and that turned out to need nothing more than a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply.

If another founder asked us before trying this, we would tell them three things. Pick the one or two metrics you will actually watch before you start, not after. Keep the trial a fixed length so you are forced to make a real decision at the end instead of drifting. And do not introduce it internally as a perk or a reward — frame it as an operating change you are testing, because that is what it is, and it changes how seriously people treat the data.

Six months in, we would do it again. But we are wary of anyone who tells you productivity and revenue both jump. For us, one moved and one didn't.
