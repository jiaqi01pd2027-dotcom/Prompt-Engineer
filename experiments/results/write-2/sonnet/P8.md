Six months ago we moved Northstar Mentors from a five-day week to four. Fridays off, same pay, no change to client rates.

I get asked about this roughly once a week now, usually by another founder who's heard the highlight reel and wants the real numbers. So here they are.

We ran it as a fixed three-month trial first, not a permanent policy, because we wanted the option to reverse it without it feeling like a broken promise. We picked one metric before we started: mentoring sessions delivered per person per week. That's up about 8%. Revenue is flat compared with the six months before the switch. Team satisfaction is up in our internal survey, and nobody has left, though with 10 people that's a small sample to draw conclusions from.

The mistake we almost made: treating this as a perk during the trial, which is the fastest way to make people feel guilty about using it. We fixed that language before we extended it and started calling it what it is, an operating change we're testing, not a gift.

The only real friction was client-facing. Some clients expect Friday responses. A shared calendar note and a clearer auto-reply solved it in about a week. That's the whole list of problems. No productivity collapse, no client exodus, no secret costs we're not mentioning.

If you're a founder considering this: pick your metric before you start, set a trial length and stick to it, and don't sell it internally as a reward.
