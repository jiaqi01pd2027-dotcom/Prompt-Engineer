Northstar Mentors is a 10-person, fully remote developer-mentorship company. Six months ago we moved from a five-day week to four: Fridays off, same pay. We got there after a three-month trial, which we then extended because the numbers held up.

Here's what went up. Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week rose about 8%. Our internal satisfaction survey improved, and nobody has left the company since we made the switch permanent.

Here's what didn't. Revenue was flat compared with the six months before the switch. Not up, not down. If you're reading founder posts about the four-day week hoping someone will admit that part, there it is. As for problems, we didn't run into anything we hadn't already braced for. The one real friction point was clients expecting responses on Fridays. A shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply took care of it.

If another founder asked us before trying this, we'd say three things. Decide the metric you're going to judge it by before you start, not after, so you're not just picking whichever number looks good in six months. Keep the trial a fixed length, and actually end it on schedule rather than quietly letting it run forever because everyone's happier. And don't introduce it internally as a perk. Frame it as an operating change with a real cost and a real bet attached, because that's what it is, and your team will take it more seriously if you do.

We got more delivered per person, kept revenue exactly where it was, and lost no one. What would have to be true at your company before you'd run this same trial?
