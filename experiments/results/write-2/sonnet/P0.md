Six months ago we moved Northstar Mentors to a four-day week. Same pay, Fridays off. We'd trialed it for three months first, then decided to keep going.

We're small — 10 people, 6 mentors, 2 in operations, 2 founders — fully remote, so a lot of the usual objections (coverage, in-person meetings, retail hours) didn't apply to us. But the core question was the same one any founder would ask: does the work still get done.

Here's what we found after six months:

Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week are up about 8%. Revenue is flat versus the six months before the switch — not up, not down. Internal satisfaction scores went up, and nobody has left.

The only real friction was client-facing: some clients expected a response on Fridays. We fixed it with a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply. That was the whole list of problems — nothing we hadn't expected going in.

If you're a founder considering this, three things helped us:

Pick the metric you'll watch before you start, not after. We watched sessions-per-person, not hours worked.

Keep the trial a fixed length. Three months, then a real decision — not an open-ended "let's see how it goes."

Don't sell it internally as a perk. Frame it as an operating change you're testing, the same way you'd test a new pricing model or a new tool. That framing changes how seriously the team treats it, and how honestly they'll tell you if it's not working.

We didn't do this to be generous. We did it because we thought the work would still get done in four days, and we wanted a clean way to find out.

What's the metric you'd want to see before you'd try this in your own company?
