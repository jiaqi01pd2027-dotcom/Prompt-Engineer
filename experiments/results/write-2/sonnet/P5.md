Six months ago we moved Northstar Mentors, all ten of us, to a four-day week. Same pay, Fridays off. We ran it as a fixed three-month trial first, then extended it, and I want to be honest about what actually happened rather than what I hoped would happen.

Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week went up about 8%. Revenue stayed flat compared with the prior six months, not up, flat. Nobody has left, and our internal satisfaction survey moved in the right direction. The only real friction was client-facing: a few clients expected Friday availability, so we added a shared calendar note and a clean auto-reply. That was the whole fix.

What surprised me was how unsurprising it was. We didn't discover some hidden reserve of productivity, and we didn't quietly work six days to make five days' worth of output happen. People seem to have compressed the same work into less time and stopped treating Friday as a leftover day for meetings nobody wanted anyway.

If you're a founder considering this, my advice: pick the metric you'll watch before you start, not after. We watched sessions per mentor and client complaints. Keep the trial a fixed length so you're not negotiating with yourself every few weeks about whether to stop. And don't pitch it internally as a perk. We told the team it was an operating experiment we might reverse, not a gift, and I think that framing is why nobody got precious about it when we hit rough patches.

We're keeping it. Would flat revenue for six months be an acceptable price for your team to pay, or is that the number that would make you pull the plug?
