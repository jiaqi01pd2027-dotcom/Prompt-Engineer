We moved Northstar Mentors, our 10-person remote developer-mentorship team, from a five-day to a four-day week six months ago, Fridays off with no pay change, after we ran it as a fixed three-month trial and then decided to extend it.

Mentoring sessions delivered per person per week are up about 8% since the switch. Our internal survey shows satisfaction is up too, and nobody has left the team in that time.

Revenue has stayed flat compared with the six months before the switch, which for us counts as a win rather than a disappointment. The only real friction was client-facing: Fridays needed a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply so nobody was left wondering why a mentor had gone quiet. Once we set that up, the problem didn't come back.

If I were telling another founder before they start, I'd say three things. Pick the metric you're going to watch before day one, not after, so you're not rationalizing the result later. Set a fixed length for the trial and stick to it, extending only after you look at the data. And don't pitch it internally as a perk or a reward, because that framing makes it fragile the first time results dip. Treat it as an operating change you're testing, the same way you'd test a new pricing model or a new hiring process.

Six months in, the main lesson is that the change forced us to be more deliberate about what actually needed five days versus what was just filling five days.
