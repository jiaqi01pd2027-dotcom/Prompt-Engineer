We moved our 10-person team to a 4-day work week six months ago. Fridays off, same pay. Here's what actually happened, no spin.

Northstar Mentors is fully remote, 6 mentors, 2 ops, 2 founders. We run developer mentorship, so our real output is sessions delivered, not hours logged.

We piloted it for 3 months first, watching one number going in: mentoring sessions per person per week. It went up about 8%. Six months in, that held. Revenue is flat versus the six months before the switch, which we count as a win, not a disappointment — flat revenue for one less day of work is a productivity gain, not a wash. Team satisfaction is up in our internal survey, and nobody has left. That last part matters more than it sounds like it should.

The problems were the boring kind. Clients occasionally needed a session on a Friday, so we put a note on the shared calendar and set a clear auto-reply. That's the whole list. No client relationships broke, no deadlines got missed, nothing we hadn't already braced for.

Three things I'd tell another founder before you try this:

Pick your one metric before you start, not after. If you decide what "working" means once you already like the answer, you're not measuring anything.

Keep the trial a fixed length and actually treat the end date as a decision point, not a formality.

Don't sell it internally as a perk. Frame it as an operating change you're testing, because that's what it is, and it's the frame that lets you reverse it without it feeling like a betrayal.

We extended it because the number held up, not because everyone liked having Fridays off.
