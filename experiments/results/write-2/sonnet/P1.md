Six months ago we moved Northstar Mentors, a 10-person fully remote developer-mentorship company, to a 4-day work week. Same pay, Fridays off. We ran it as a 3-month trial first, then extended it. Here's what we actually learned.

Mentoring sessions delivered per person, per week, went up about 8%. Revenue stayed flat compared with the six months before the switch — not a windfall, but not a cost either. Our internal satisfaction survey improved, and nobody has left the company since we started. The only real friction was client-facing: some clients expected us reachable on Fridays, so we added a shared calendar note and a clear auto-reply. That was the whole fix.

Three things I'd tell another founder considering this:

Pick your metric before you start. We watched sessions-per-person and revenue from day one. Without a number you agreed on in advance, you'll end up arguing about vibes three months in.

Keep the trial fixed-length. We committed to three months, no early verdict either way. That stopped both the skeptics and the cheerleaders from calling it a success or failure in week two.

Don't sell it internally as a perk. We framed it as an operating change we were testing, not a reward for good behavior. That distinction mattered — it kept the conversation on output, not on gratitude, and made it easier to have a real discussion if the numbers had gone the other way.

We got lucky in some ways — a mentorship business has fairly measurable output. I don't know how cleanly this maps onto every team.
