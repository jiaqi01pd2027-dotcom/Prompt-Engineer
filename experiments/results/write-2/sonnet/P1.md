Six months ago we cut our team from five days to four. Same pay, Fridays off, no catch.

We're Northstar Mentors — 10 people, fully remote, we pair developers with mentors. Small enough that a bad experiment would have been obvious fast.

We ran a 3-month trial first, decided in advance what we'd measure, and extended it because the numbers held up. Here's what six months actually looks like:

Mentoring sessions delivered per person, per week: up about 8%. Revenue: flat, comparing the six months before and after. Team satisfaction, from our internal survey: up. Nobody has left.

Problems: fewer than we braced for, and the one that showed up was minor — some clients kept booking Friday sessions out of habit. We fixed it with a shared calendar note and a clearer auto-reply. That's the whole list.

Two things I'd tell another founder before they try this.

First, pick your metric before you start, not after. We watched sessions-per-person and revenue from day one, so "it's working" was a number, not a vibe.

Second, don't pitch it internally as a perk. We treated it as an operating change with a fixed trial and a real chance we'd reverse it. That framing is what let us look at the data honestly instead of defending a decision we'd already made.

We're not claiming a four-day week is free productivity for everyone. It was flat-to-slightly-up for us, in a business built on scheduled one-on-one sessions.
