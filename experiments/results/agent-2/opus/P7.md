## Reasoning

**What the brief doesn't say.** Who "customers" are (org admins, every seat, resellers with sub-accounts?). What "usage" means (the metered unit that drives billing, or activity like logins and feature adoption?). Why now — a billing-dispute problem, a support-ticket problem, or an upsell play? Whether an authoritative usage record already exists or has to be built. How fresh the numbers must be. Whether they must reconcile to the invoice. History depth, granularity, export, alerts, and where the page lives.

**Gaps that move the plan most.** Three:
1. **Is there a trustworthy usage source today?** If metering has to be built, that's 70% of the work and the "dashboard" is the small part.
2. **Must the numbers match the invoice?** "Roughly right" and "billing-grade" are different projects — the second brings reconciliation, immutability of closed periods, and a much heavier accuracy bar.
3. **Freshness.** Daily rollups are a batch job. Near-real-time is a streaming architecture and roughly doubles scope.

Tenancy/permissions is a fourth: sub-orgs and resellers quietly turn one query into an authorization design.

**Smallest version that satisfies the sentence.** One page inside the existing app, scoped to the org, showing the current billing period's total for the single metric that drives the bill, plus a daily bar chart for the last 30 days and a plain-language "as of" timestamp. Read-only, refreshed hourly from a pre-aggregated table.

**Left out on purpose.** Real-time, alerts and thresholds, per-user or per-endpoint drilldown, arbitrary date ranges, CSV/API export, forecasting or projected spend, multi-metric views, anomaly detection, embedded invoices.

**Where it slips.** Definition disagreement — sales, billing, and engineering each have a different answer to "what counts as one unit," and the argument surfaces in week 4, not week 1. Second: source data with gaps, duplicates, or late arrivals that nobody knew about until it was displayed. Third: the support load that starts the day the numbers become visible.

## Plan:

**Assumption this plan runs on:** an authoritative usage event source already exists. If M0 shows it doesn't, this becomes a metering project and the estimate roughly doubles — flag it that week, don't absorb it.

**Team:** 2 engineers (1 backend, 1 frontend), design part-time. **Duration:** ~6 weeks to GA.

| Milestone | Week | Exit criteria |
|---|---|---|
| **M0 — Definition lock** | 1 | One written definition of the metric, signed off by billing + support. Source-of-truth table identified. Known data gaps documented. Go/no-go on the assumption above. |
| **M1 — Aggregation** | 2–3 | Hourly job producing per-org daily rollups, backfilled 90 days. Handles late and duplicate events idempotently. |
| **M2 — API** | 3 | Authenticated, org-scoped endpoint returning period total + daily series. P95 under 300ms for the largest tenant. |
| **M3 — UI** | 4 | Single page in the existing app: period total, 30-day chart, "as of" timestamp, empty and error states. |
| **M4 — Accuracy validation** | 5 | Dashboard totals reconcile to billing within an agreed tolerance across the 20 largest orgs, with variances explained. Support briefed. |
| **M5 — Staged rollout** | 6 | Internal dogfood, then 10% of orgs, then all. Support ticket volume monitored as the rollout gate. |

**Decisions needed before M1 starts:** the metric definition (M0 output), whether it must reconcile to the invoice, history depth, and who can see the page (admins only vs all seats).

**Risks**

- **Definition drift** *(high likelihood, high impact)* — the metric gets relitigated mid-build. M0 exists to force it early; treat a change after M1 as a scope reset, not a tweak.
- **Dirty source data** *(medium/high)* — gaps or duplicates only become visible once displayed. M4 is deliberately a full week; don't let it be the buffer that gets eaten.
- **Reconciliation creep** *(medium/high)* — "close enough" quietly becomes "must match the invoice." Decide this in M0; it changes M1 and M4 substantially.
- **Support load post-launch** *(high likelihood, medium impact)* — every discrepancy becomes a ticket. Brief support in M4 and gate the rollout on ticket volume.
- **Large-tenant query cost** *(medium)* — mitigated by pre-aggregation in M1; verify against real production volumes, not seed data.

**Cut lines if week 4 is late:** drop the chart and ship the period total alone; drop the 90-day backfill to 30. Do not cut M4.
