Scope (in)
- Usage dashboard page inside the existing authenticated customer web app, scoped to the signed-in customer's own account.
- Headline metrics for the current billing period: total usage to date, plan allowance and percent consumed, change vs. previous period.
- One time-series chart (daily usage) plus one top-N breakdown table by a single dimension (e.g. product/endpoint or seat/user).
- Date range control with fixed presets: current period, previous period, last 30 days.
- CSV export of exactly what the current view shows.
- Batch aggregation pipeline that rolls existing raw usage events into a daily per-account, per-dimension summary table, with a documented freshness SLA and a visible "data as of <timestamp>" label.
- Access control: usage visible only to authenticated members of the owning account; admin/member gating reused from existing roles if they exist.
- Standard states: loading, empty, no-data-yet (new account), partial-data, and error.
- Product analytics on the dashboard itself (views, range selected, export clicks) to prioritize v2.
- Responsive layout for desktop and tablet; usable but not optimized on phone.

Non-goals
- Real-time or streaming usage; v1 is daily rollups only.
- Usage alerts, thresholds, budget caps, or notifications of any kind.
- Cost, spend, invoice, or billing reconciliation figures.
- Custom report builder, saved views, arbitrary pivots, or multi-dimension drilldown.
- Public usage API, webhooks, or scheduled email/report delivery.
- Cross-account, org-hierarchy, reseller, or internal all-customer views.
- Backfill of history older than the raw-event retention window.
- Native mobile app, localization, white-labeling, or embedding in customer apps.
- Changes to how usage is metered or defined; v1 reports the existing metric as-is.

Assumptions and open questions
- (assumption) Raw usage events already exist in a queryable store with account ID, timestamp, and at least one breakdown dimension; no new instrumentation is required.
- (assumption) Daily freshness (data through end of previous UTC day) is acceptable for v1.
- (assumption) The dashboard lives in the existing web app and reuses its auth, nav, design system, and charting library, so no new frontend infrastructure is needed.
- (assumption) Plan allowance/limit per account is retrievable from the billing or entitlements system via an existing internal API.
- (assumption) Team is roughly two engineers (one backend, one frontend) plus part-time design and PM.
- (question) Which usage metric is the headline number, and does the customer-facing definition match how we bill? If they differ, which one do we show?
- (question) What is the breakdown dimension customers actually want first — product/endpoint, seat/user, project, or region?
- (question) How much history must v1 show (13 months for annual comparison, or 90 days), and does raw retention cover it?
- (question) Do usage periods follow the billing cycle (which may start mid-month) or calendar months?
- (question) Is per-seat/per-user attribution considered sensitive, and does it need admin-only gating or an org-level privacy setting?
- (question) What is the largest account by event volume, and what query latency is acceptable at that size?
- (question) Do we need an audit trail of who viewed or exported usage data for compliance?

| 5 | Export, analytics, hardening | CSV export, product analytics events, load test against the largest account, accessibility pass | 3-4 days |

Risks
- Reported usage does not match the invoice, generating support tickets and eroding trust: lock the metric definition in Milestone 1, reconcile the dashboard against real invoices for at least 20 accounts before rollout, and label the dashboard as an estimate with a freshness timestamp.
- Raw event volume makes queries slow or expensive for the largest accounts: pre-aggregate into daily rollups rather than querying raw events, and load-test against the top-decile account in Milestone 5 before committing to a latency target.
- The breakdown dimension we ship is not the one customers asked for, so the feature lands flat: validate the choice with 3-5 design-partner interviews during Milestone 1, and instrument the dashboard so v2 prioritization comes from data.
- Missing or malformed historical events produce gaps and visible wrong numbers: add data-quality checks to the rollup job, render known gaps explicitly instead of as zeros, and cap the displayed history at the window we can verify.
- Per-user attribution exposes information some customers treat as sensitive: default seat-level breakdowns to admin-only and confirm with legal/privacy before general availability.
