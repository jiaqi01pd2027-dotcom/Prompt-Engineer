Scope (in)
- Current-month usage per metric shown against the account's plan limit (numeric value + simple visual indicator, e.g. progress bar), for the logged-in admin's own account only.
- Trailing six months of usage trend per metric, shown as a chart.
- CSV download of the monthly usage numbers (the same monthly figures shown on the dashboard).
- Email alert sent to the account when a metric reaches 80% of its plan limit — the only notification in v1.
- New daily rollup/aggregation table(s) built from the existing `usage_events` table (none exist today); dashboard reads from the rollup, not raw events.
- Daily refresh: rollup job runs after the billing pipeline finishes (03:00 UTC), so the dashboard reflects data through the prior day.
- Dashboard lives behind the existing web app's session login; an admin sees only their own account's data. No new auth system.
- Runs on existing infrastructure only — no new infra spend.
- Soft launch to 20 pilot accounts ahead of full launch to all ~1,200 paid accounts, before the November 2 pricing change.

Non-goals
- No billing or invoice display.
- No per-user breakdown within an account (account-level only).
- No public API.
- No mobile app.
- No custom date ranges (fixed: current month + trailing 6 months).

Assumptions and open questions
- (question) Where do per-metric plan limits live today (which table/service)? Required to compute "% of limit" and the 80% alert threshold — this is a blocking unknown.
- (assumption) Plan limits are already available in a queryable form (DB table or internal API) that backend can join against `usage_events` by account.
- (assumption) "Monthly numbers" in the CSV are the same aggregated monthly figures shown on the dashboard, not a raw event-level export.
- (assumption) The 80% alert fires once per metric per billing period on first crossing, not repeatedly, to avoid spamming the admin.
- (assumption) The alert email goes to the account admin's email already on file in the existing app; no new contact-collection flow is built.
- (question) Does the existing app already have a transactional email system the alert job can call, or does email sending need to be built from scratch? Plan assumes one exists and is reused.
- (question) What are the exact `metric` values in `usage_events`, and do they differ by plan? Needed for chart/table labeling and CSV column names.
- (assumption) "Six months of trend" means six trailing calendar months (including the current, partial month) at monthly granularity, not daily.
- (question) How are the 20 pilot accounts selected, and is an account allowlist/feature-flag mechanism already available, or does this project need to build a minimal one? Plan assumes a lightweight config-based allowlist is sufficient.
- (assumption) The designer's one week is used entirely in week 1; no further design time is budgeted, so visual decisions are locked after week 1.

Risks
- No rollup table exists today and query/index performance for 1,200 accounts is unproven: validate the rollup approach with a real data volume spike during week 1 (Milestone 1) rather than discovering scaling issues during Milestone 2.
- Plan-limit data source is unknown and may not be readily queryable, which blocks both the usage-vs-limit display and the 80% alert: confirm this explicitly in week 1 as a go/no-go gate before backend work begins in Milestone 2.
- Email alerting is new functionality with unclear reuse of existing email infrastructure and dedup logic: scope it to the simplest version (once per metric per billing period, reusing existing email sending if available) and build/test it in Milestone 4, not at the end.
- Only two weeks separate pilot launch and the hard November 2 deadline, leaving little slack to fix issues found with real customer data: treat the Milestone 5 pilot launch as a strict checklist-gated event, and reserve Milestone 6 for fixes only, with no new scope added.
- Single engineer per discipline (one backend, one frontend) means any illness or blocker stalls that entire track with no backup: lock the API contract in week 1, run daily syncs between the two engineers, and escalate any slip to the PM immediately rather than absorbing it silently.
