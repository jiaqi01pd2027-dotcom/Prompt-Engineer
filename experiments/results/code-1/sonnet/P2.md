**Implementation approach**: two regexes.

```python
_WEEKS_RE = re.compile(r'^P(\d+)W$')
_FULL_RE = re.compile(
    r'^P(?!$)'
    r'(?:(\d+)Y)?'
    r'(?:(\d+)M)?'
    r'(?:(\d+)D)?'
    r'(?:T(?=\d)(?:(\d+)H)?(?:(\d+)M)?(?:(\d+(?:\.\d+)?)S)?)?$'
)
```

- `P2W` matches the weeks-only form and is scored in isolation (weeks don't combine with Y/M/D per the spec's "standalone weeks").
- The full form enforces canonical ISO-8601 ordering (Y, M, D, then T, H, M, S), requires a `T` before any time unit (`(?=\d)` after `T` rejects a dangling `PT`/`P1YT`), and only lets the seconds group carry a decimal point — `\d+` (no dot) for every other unit, so `P1.5Y`, `PT1.5H`, etc. fail to match and raise `ValueError`.
- `P(?!$)` rejects a bare `"P"`.
- Seconds are accumulated as `float(seconds)`; the one bug I caught during testing was that the running total stayed a `float` even when the fractional part summed to a whole number (e.g. `P1Y2M3DT4H5M6S` produced `36993906.0` instead of `int`). Fixed by converting the total back to `int` via `total.is_integer()` before returning, so whole-second results are `int` and only genuinely fractional results (e.g. `PT1.5S` → `1.5`) stay `float`, matching the `float | int` signature.

Test coverage includes the five spec examples, individual/combined component subsets, zero values, fractional-second edge cases, and the required invalid cases (no leading `P`, empty `P`/`PT`, time unit without `T`, fractions outside seconds, unknown characters, wrong component order, weeks mixed with other units, non-string input, negative sign).
